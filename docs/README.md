# OctoAcme Process Documentation

## Process Summary

OctoAcme uses a structured project management lifecycle to move work from idea to continuous improvement. Teams begin in initiation by defining the business problem, success metrics, stakeholder alignment, initial risks, and a high-level timeline. Once an initiative is approved, planning turns that direction into a prioritized backlog, agreed milestones, documented dependencies, a Definition of Done, and an initial QA approach so delivery can start with shared expectations.

Execution and tracking focus on predictable delivery rhythms and visible work management. Delivery teams use project boards to move work from backlog through review, QA, and done while maintaining a regular cadence of standups, weekly delivery syncs, and sprint or milestone demos. Progress, blockers, and dependencies are escalated through a clear path from the team to the Project Manager, Product Lead, and sponsor when business impact requires broader coordination.

Roles are intentionally cross-functional and explicit. Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, and stakeholder communication; Product Managers define outcomes, priorities, and success measures; Developers implement, review, and test solutions; QA and testing partners validate acceptance criteria and release readiness; and stakeholders provide direction, approvals, and feedback. Communication is anchored in a single source of truth, reinforced with weekly or milestone-based updates, risk register reviews, and incident communication when urgent issues arise.

Quality assurance is embedded throughout the lifecycle rather than reserved for the end of a project. During delivery, teams are expected to keep pull requests reviewable, link work to issues and acceptance criteria, and complete CI checks such as automated tests, linting, and security scanning before merge. Before release, OctoAcme emphasizes smoke-test readiness, rollback planning, post-deployment verification, and stakeholder announcements; after each sprint, release, or incident, retrospectives convert lessons learned into tracked action items that support continuous improvement.
